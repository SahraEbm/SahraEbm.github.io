About

Sahra Ebrahimmagham is a semiconductor device researcher specializing in GaN HEMTs, physics-based compact modeling, and energy-efficient hardware for sustainable artificial intelligence.

Professional Summary

Semiconductor device researcher specializing in Gallium Nitride (GaN) HEMTs, physics-based compact modeling, and energy-efficient hardware for sustainable artificial intelligence. Currently leading two complementary research teams focused on advanced power semiconductor devices and brain-inspired computing architectures. Passionate about building the semiconductor foundations required for next-generation energy-efficient AI systems.

Leadership & Research Experience

Research Lead – Dual-Track Innovation Program

Independent Research Initiative | 2025 – Present

Leading two complementary research teams focused on solving the energy efficiency bottleneck of future AI systems:

Team 1: Advanced Semiconductor Devices (Power Infrastructure)

  • Leading research on next-generation wide-bandgap technologies with a strong focus on GaN High Electron Mobility Transistors (HEMTs).
  • Developing physics-based compact models (Verilog-A) for GaN HEMTs with Gate Field-Plate and dual Gate + Source Field-Plate structures.
  • Targeting high power density, superior conversion efficiency, and thermal performance required for AI data centers and high-performance computing systems.

Team 2: Brain-Inspired Computing (Architectural Paradigm)

  • Leading exploration of event-driven computing architectures inspired by biological neural networks.
  • Investigating how neuromorphic principles can overcome the energy limitations of conventional processors.
  • Bridging semiconductor device physics with bio-inspired system architectures for sustainable AI hardware.

Research Team Lead – Next-Generation Wide-Bandgap Transistors

2021 – 2024

Led a research team focused on the development and modeling of next-generation wide-bandgap transistors, with particular emphasis on GaN HEMT device physics, field-plate engineering, and physics-based compact modeling for power and high-frequency applications.
